Google Business Profile: A Practical Guide for Nepali Businesses

Dikshan Gautam8 min read

For most local businesses — restaurants, clinics, consultancies, shops, hotels — Google Business Profile is the single highest-leverage thing you can set up. It's free, and it's often the first thing a potential customer sees.

1. Claim and verify the listing

Verification usually happens by postcard, phone or email, depending on the business category. Skipping this step means someone else — or nobody — controls what customers see about you.

2. Get the basics exactly right

Business name, correct category, accurate address or service area, real phone number, and consistent hours. Inconsistent details across the web quietly damage trust with both customers and Google.

3. Add real photos, regularly

Interior, exterior, team, products or work samples — whatever represents the business honestly. Avoid stock photography; customers can usually tell.

4. Use posts and updates

Businesses that post updates and offers consistently tend to look more active and trustworthy than ones with a listing that hasn't changed in years.

A profile with accurate categories, real photos and a steady flow of reviews will usually outperform a nicer-looking but static setup.

5. Build a simple review habit

Don't buy reviews — it's against policy and easy to spot. Ask satisfied customers directly, right after a good experience, and reply to every review, including negative ones, professionally.

6. Connect it to your website

Consistent name, address and phone details, a link to the profile, and website content that matches what the profile promises.

Common mistakes

  • Choosing a broad category instead of the most specific one available
  • Leaving the profile half-completed for months
  • Ignoring negative reviews instead of responding

A well-maintained free listing regularly outperforms a paid ad for local "near me" searches.
