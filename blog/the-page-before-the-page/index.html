The Page Before the Page

Before someone reaches your offer page, the page, search result or question that led them there already shaped their expectation.

Dikshan Gautam · Digital strategy

Before someone reaches your offer page, the page, search result or question that led them there already shaped their expectation.

  • A user searching high-level comparisons is not ready to book a sales call.
  • The "Page Before the Page" acts as an orientation lobby where the visitor understands their options and self-selects their path.
  • Hub pages solve Google's topical authority requirements by cleanly interlinking sub-topics.

1. The Premature Conversion Fallacy

A common mistake made by agency web designers in Nepal is trying to close the sale on every single URL. You visit a page searching for "How to verify TU Equivalence," and you are immediately greeted by a massive modal: "Talk to an Admission Counselor Now!"

This creates immediate cognitive resistance. The visitor is in exploratory mode. Demanding their phone number and email address before giving them any useful information signals that you care about your sales pipeline more than their situation.

2. The Role of the Orientation Hub

An orientation page does not sell; it organizes. It provides the taxonomy of the problem. For example, on the Education Desk, the main /education/ hub does not ask students to enroll in anything. Instead, it offers 6 distinct portals corresponding to their exact life stage: +2 selection, undergraduate degrees, professional short courses, admissions essays, scholarships, and international study.

Once the visitor enters the specific subpage (such as /education/bachelors/ ), they are given objective evaluation criteria. Only after they understand the landscape do they encounter a natural recommendation to reach out for a consultation.

3. The SEO Advantage of Topic Silos

From an algorithm perspective, Google loves this architectural clarity. A hub page linked symmetrically to 4–6 tight, non-overlapping child pages creates a clear topical cluster. Google's web crawler can easily crawl the entire branch, recognize the semantic relationships, and award higher domain authority for the whole topic.
