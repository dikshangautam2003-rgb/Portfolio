SEO for Nepali Businesses: Where Should You Actually Start?

Dikshan Gautam8 min read

Most small and mid-sized businesses in Nepal don't need a 40-point SEO checklist. They need three or four things done properly, in the right order. Here's where I'd start.

1. Make sure Google can actually find and read your site

Before worrying about rankings, check the basics: does your site load on a phone in a few seconds, is there one clear page for each main service or product, and can Google Search Console see your pages without errors?

2. Research how your customers actually search

Search behaviour in Nepal is rarely pure English. People search in English, Nepali, and Romanized Nepali — often mixing all three in the same session. A business that only optimises for the English term is missing real demand.

3. Fix on-page basics before writing new content

Titles, headings and descriptions that actually describe the page, internal links between related pages, and one clear call to action per page. Unglamorous, but usually worth more than another blog post.

If you can only do one thing this month: check whether your homepage title and description actually describe what you do and where — most don't.

4. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

For any business with a physical location or service area, this often matters more than the website itself. Covered in detail in the Google Business Profile guide.

5. Build content around real questions

Write the page you'd want to read if you were the customer trying to decide. Answer the actual question in the first two sentences, then add detail.

What I'd avoid

  • Buying backlinks from unrelated, low-quality sites
  • Publishing content nobody is searching for, just to "keep the blog active"
  • Ignoring page speed and mobile experience

SEO in Nepal isn't fundamentally different from SEO anywhere else — but local intent, language mix, and a less saturated competitive landscape mean a small, focused effort can go further than in a mature market.
