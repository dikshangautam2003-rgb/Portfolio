A small system for student research

Education desk08 Jan 20257 min read

For Nepali students comparing courses, colleges and scholarship options, a calmer way to begin.

Research becomes overwhelming when every tab feels equally important. A better approach is to separate the search into decisions: what you want to study, where it can lead, what it costs, and which requirements are real.

Keep one working page

Use a single note for the shortlist. Give each option the same fields: institution, course, intake, eligibility, estimated cost, deadline, source link and one unresolved question. A consistent shape makes differences visible.

Start with official pages wherever possible. College pages and government notices change; save the date you checked a requirement rather than relying on a screenshot with no context.

Research is not a race

The point is not to collect the most options. It is to reach a small set that you can compare with enough care to choose one. If a requirement is unclear, that is useful information — mark it and ask the institution directly.

A shortlist is valuable when it makes a decision smaller.

The education desk is built around that idea: fewer vague promises, more navigable information for students and families.
