A Small System for Student Research

A practical way for students to compare courses, colleges and study options without getting buried in tabs and conflicting advice.

Dikshan Gautam · Digital strategy

A practical way for students to compare courses, colleges and study options without getting buried in tabs and conflicting advice.

  • Step 1: Consult official government curriculum portals (CDC / NEB / TU) before reading any college website.
  • Step 2: Build an objective decision spreadsheet comparing tuition, hidden fees, faculty ratios, and location.
  • Step 3: Conduct informal interviews with alumni who graduated 1–2 years ago on LinkedIn.
  • Step 4: Physically inspect laboratories and libraries during ordinary class hours.

1. The Information Fog in Nepali Education

Between the month of Ashadh and Bhadra in Nepal, every billboard in Kathmandu is covered with college advertisements claiming to be the "Best in the Country." Television spots, sponsored social media reels, and celebrity endorsements create an overwhelming sensory overload.

In this environment, a student who relies on commercial marketing will almost certainly make a decision based on superficial factors: a sleek building façade, a brand-new cafeteria, or a free tablet computer bundled with enrollment.

2. The Decision Spreadsheet

To cut through the fog, create a simple spreadsheet with five columns:

  • Institutional Affiliation: (e.g. TU constituent, TU affiliated, KU, PU, or foreign university with CDC equivalence certificate).
  • True Total Financial Outlay: (tuition + semester exam fees + laboratory charges + library security deposits over 4 years).
  • Core Faculty Continuity: How many full-time PhD or Master's faculty are permanent vs. part-time guest teachers?
  • Campus Laboratory Access: Can you work in the labs outside of scheduled class hours?
  • Direct Alumni Feedback: Contact two recent alumni on LinkedIn. Ask one simple question: "What do you wish you had known before enrolling?"

3. Why I Maintain the Education Desk

The Education Desk on this portfolio site is my contribution to clearing this fog. By using structured data, clean typography, and zero affiliate sponsorships, it provides an objective, plain-language reference that respects the dignity of Nepali students and their families.
