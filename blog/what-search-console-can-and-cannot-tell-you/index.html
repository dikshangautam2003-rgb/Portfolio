What Search Console Can and Cannot Tell You

Google Search Console gives useful search data, but understanding what the numbers actually mean matters more than chasing the dashboard.

Dikshan Gautam · SEO & search

Google Search Console gives useful search data, but understanding what the numbers actually mean matters more than chasing the dashboard.

  • An impression simply means your URL was loaded somewhere on the search result page — even on position 9 below the fold where the user never scrolled.
  • Average Position blends diverse queries and locations together, hiding massive swings in localized queries.
  • The high-ROI opportunity lives in queries with high impressions but low CTR in positions 4–12.

1. The Illusion of the Impression

In Google Search Console, an impression is registered whenever a URL snippet appears in the search results viewed by a user. Crucially, if your result appears at position #8 on a desktop screen, and the user scrolls down, that counts as an impression. If your result is part of an image carousel or sitelink cluster, it can record an impression even if the user barely glances in that direction.

When clients tell me, "Our impressions doubled this month but our inquiries stayed flat," my first diagnostic step is examining the query distribution. If the new impressions were driven by broad informational queries from users across the world seeking definitions rather than local clients seeking a provider in Nepal, the traffic has zero commercial utility.

2. Why Average Position is a Dangerous Metric

Google Search Console calculates Average Position by averaging the position of your URL across every single query it ranked for over the selected timeframe. Consider this scenario:

  • You rank #2 for your core transactional keyword: "educational consultancy in kathmandu" (100 impressions).
  • A blog post ranks #85 for a massive generic keyword: "study tips" (5,000 impressions).

Your blended average position will show as ~83.4, making it look like your site is performing terribly, when in reality your core money page is dominating the local 3-pack and generating clients every day. Never evaluate site health using sitewide average position; always filter by individual canonical URLs.

3. The Sweet Spot: Positions 4 through 12

The highest return on investment in SEO auditing does not come from trying to rank brand new pages for impossibly competitive terms. It comes from mining your existing Search Console queries for URLs ranking between position 4 and position 12.

Google already considers your page relevant for these terms, but you are losing the top 3 clicks because of subtle technical or editorial friction: a weak title tag, missing subheadings answering specific sub-questions, or slow mobile loading times. By rewriting the title, adding targeted Schema FAQs, and tightening the opening paragraph, you can push a position 7 result to position 2 within three weeks.

4. What GSC Cannot Tell You

Search Console cuts off at the click. It cannot tell you whether the user found the answer, whether your contact form was broken on iOS Safari, or whether the visitor bounced after 3 seconds because the font was too small. That is where GA4 behavioral flow, heatmaps, and plain-language usability testing must take over.
