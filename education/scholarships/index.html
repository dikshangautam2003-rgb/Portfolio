Three practical guides

Quota allocations, dignity-first essays and keeping your award every semester.

01Mapping domestic & global scholarship types for Nepal

Scholarships are divided into three primary categories: government mandated quotas, institutional merit awards and international donor fellowships.

  • MOEST & municipality +2 quota (10%): Administered through local ward/municipality screening examinations. Targets government school graduates, low-income families and under-represented communities. Covers full tuition at private +2 colleges.
  • University merit scholarships: TU, KU and PU grant top-rank entrance exam scorers full or partial semester tuition fee discounts.
  • US undergraduate need-based aid: Many US liberal arts colleges meet 100% of demonstrated financial need for international students via CSS Profile evaluations.
  • Prestigious global fellowships: Programs like Erasmus Mundus (Europe), Australia Awards and Fulbright (Master's level) provide fully-funded tuition, airfare and monthly living stipends.

02How to write a compelling financial aid essay

A great financial aid essay communicates dignity, responsibility and clear educational commitment — rather than despair or helplessness.

  • Specific financial context: Quantify your household financial situation objectively (family income, agricultural dependencies, medical expenses, local cost realities in Nepal).
  • Demonstrated resourcefulness: Explain what you have already done to support your education (tutoring younger students, freelance projects, managing family budgets).
  • Targeted return on investment: Explain specifically how removing the tuition burden unlocks your ability to focus on research, campus service and community initiatives.

03Scholarship retention: how to keep your award every year

Winning a scholarship is step one; retaining it across four years requires vigilance regarding academic standing requirements.

  • Minimum GPA benchmark: Understand whether your scholarship requires maintaining a 3.0, 3.2 or 3.5 cumulative GPA each semester.
  • Full-time credit load: Most awards strictly require taking at least 15 credit hours (5 full subjects) each semester without dropping classes.
  • Disciplinary & attendance standing: Minimum 80% classroom attendance is standard for maintaining tuition waivers.
Keep a personal compliance checklist at the start of every semester: GPA, credits, attendance. A scholarship lost in year three is far harder to replace than one protected with a little bookkeeping.
