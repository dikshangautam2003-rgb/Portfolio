01A plain-language guide to choosing a +2 stream

Choosing between Science, Management, Humanities and Law is not an intelligence test; it is an alignment decision based on what kind of cognitive work you enjoy and where you plan to go after Grade 12.

  • Science (Physical vs. Biological): Best for students targeting Engineering (IOE), Medicine (CEE/MBBS), Nursing, Agriculture or Pure Sciences. Requires strong conceptual math and consistent daily problem-solving. Also preserves eligibility to switch to BBA or IT later.
  • Management (Business, Accounting, Hotel Management, Computer Science): Best for students interested in Finance, Entrepreneurship, Banking, Hospitality or Business IT (BCA/BBM). Emphasizes analytical thinking, communication and commercial logic.
  • Humanities (Sociology, Mass Communication, Psychology, English): Ideal for careers in Journalism, Policy, Social Research, International Relations and Creative Arts. Demands heavy reading, critical essay writing and analytical discussion.
  • Law (Jurisprudence, Procedural Law): A dedicated path towards BA LLB, focusing on constitutional principles, statutory interpretation, debate and legal writing.
The golden rule: never pick Science solely because of social or parental pressure. A high GPA in Management or Humanities unlocks far more university scholarships and career opportunities than a struggling 2.2 GPA in Science.

02NEB exam preparation: build a week you can repeat

Cramming the night before board exams creates panic and shallow recall. Under NEB's updated grading system, continuous internal assessments and practical evaluations make up 25% to 50% of your final grade.

  • Block A — Active theory review (Monday & Tuesday): Re-read class lecture notes, summarize textbook definitions in your own words, and draft formula cheat sheets.
  • Block B — Past question solving (Wednesday & Thursday): Solve the previous 5 years of NEB board questions under timed conditions. Focus on numerical problems and multi-step derivations.
  • Block C — Practical lab records (Friday): Complete practical notebooks, lab reports and internal assignments early so they do not pile up during pre-board exams.
  • Block D — Mock test & error audit (Saturday): Complete one full 3-hour model exam paper. Score yourself strictly against the NEB answer marking scheme to identify weak spots.
Common mistake: relying solely on commercially published guidebooks without reading the government textbook. NEB examiners mark strictly according to the official curriculum objectives.

03What to check before enrolling at a +2 college

Private +2 colleges in Kathmandu spend heavily on billboard advertising, celebrity endorsements and air-conditioned lobbies. When visiting colleges, look past the marketing façade and evaluate the substance.

  • Faculty continuity: Are the teachers full-time dedicated educators, or part-time visiting lecturers rushing between three different campuses?
  • Hidden fee transparency: Demand a full itemized 2-year fee breakdown. Ask specifically about terminal exam fees, lab consumables, uniform packages, bus charges and ECA extra charges.
  • Science / computer lab equipment: Check if microscopes, reagents and computer labs are fully functional and accessible during free periods — not locked behind glass for display.
  • Student-to-teacher ratio: Avoid classrooms with 60+ students packed together where asking questions is discouraged.
  • Entrance scholarship terms: Understand the renewal conditions. Does the scholarship require maintaining an unrealistic 3.8 GPA every term, or does it apply across both Grade 11 and Grade 12?
