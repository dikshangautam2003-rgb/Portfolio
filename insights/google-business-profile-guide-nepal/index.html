Google Business Profile: A Practical Guide for Nepali Businesses

Dikshan Gautam6 min read

For most local businesses — restaurants, clinics, consultancies, shops, hotels — Google Business Profile is the single highest-leverage thing you can set up. It's free, and it's often the first thing a potential customer sees.

1. Claim and verify the listing

If you haven't claimed your business on Google yet, that's step one. Verification usually happens by postcard, phone or email, depending on the business category. Skipping this step means someone else — or nobody — controls what customers see about you.

2. Get the basics exactly right

Business name (no keyword-stuffed extras), correct category, accurate address or service area, real phone number, and consistent hours. Inconsistent information across the web (your website says one phone number, your listing says another) quietly damages trust with both customers and Google.

3. Add real photos, regularly

Listings with more recent, genuine photos tend to get more engagement. Interior, exterior, team, products or work samples — whatever represents the business honestly. Avoid stock photography here; customers can usually tell.

4. Use posts and updates

Google Business Profile lets you post updates, offers and events directly to your listing. Businesses that use this consistently tend to look more active and trustworthy than ones with a listing that hasn't changed in years.

A profile with accurate categories, real photos and a steady flow of reviews will usually outperform a profile with a nicer-looking but static setup.

5. Build a simple review habit

Don't buy reviews — it's against Google's policies and it's easy to spot. Instead, build a habit of asking satisfied customers directly, right after a good experience, with a simple link. Reply to reviews, including negative ones, professionally and specifically.

6. Connect it to your website

Your website and your Google Business Profile should reinforce each other: consistent name, address and phone details, a link from your site to the profile, and content on your site that matches what your profile promises.

Common mistakes I see

  • Choosing a broad category ("Business") instead of the most specific one available.
  • Leaving the profile unverified or half-completed for months.
  • Ignoring negative reviews instead of responding.

None of this is complicated, but it takes consistency. A well-maintained free listing regularly outperforms a paid ad for local intent searches like "near me" queries.
