How Much Does a Business Website Actually Cost in Nepal?

Dikshan Gautam6 min read

"How much does a website cost?" is a bit like asking "how much does a house cost?" — it depends heavily on what you actually need. Here's a more useful way to think about it than chasing a single number.

What actually drives the price

Three things move the number more than anything else: how many genuinely different pages/templates the site needs, whether it needs custom functionality (booking, payments, multi-language, inventory) versus a straightforward informational site, and how much original content (copy, photos) needs to be created versus supplied.

A simple, informational site

A clear homepage, a few service or product pages, an about page and a contact page — built on a solid structure with on-page SEO in place — is the right starting point for most small and mid-sized businesses. This is usually the most cost-effective option, and it's often more effective than an expensive site with the wrong priorities.

Where budget is genuinely worth spending

  • Clear information architecture — pages organised around what customers are actually trying to do.
  • On-page SEO from day one, not added later as an afterthought.
  • Mobile performance — a large share of traffic in Nepal is mobile-first.
  • A clear path to contact or enquiry on every page.

Where it's easy to overspend

Custom animations, unnecessary functionality "just in case," and template complexity that doesn't match how customers actually use the site are the most common places budget disappears without adding value.

What to ask before you get a quote

  • How many pages does the business actually need, realistically?
  • Will the site be built with SEO structure in place, or added afterward?
  • Who is responsible for content — copy and images — and is that included in the quote?
  • What happens after launch — is there a plan for updates, or is it a one-time build?

The right question isn't "what's the cheapest website I can get" or "what's the most impressive website I can afford" — it's "what does this business actually need to turn visitors into enquiries, and nothing more."
