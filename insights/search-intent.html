Search intent is more useful than the keyword.

A keyword tells you what was typed. Search intent helps you understand why it was typed.

Dikshan GautamEditorial note2026
A practical way to think about the question behind the query.

Start with the job

Every search has a job behind it. Someone may be learning, comparing, evaluating, looking for a specific page or trying to act. Two queries with similar words can have very different jobs.

Look at the results

The current search results are useful evidence. What formats dominate? Guides, product pages, category pages, videos, tools? Google is giving you clues about what it believes satisfies the query.

Build around the answer

Once the intent is clear, the page becomes easier to plan. The heading, structure, examples, internal links and call to action can all support the same job instead of competing for attention.

Use keywords as signals

Keyword volume can help prioritize, but volume alone does not tell you what a good page should do. Treat keywords as signals inside a broader research process.
