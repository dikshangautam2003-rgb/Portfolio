SEO for Nepali Businesses: Where to Start

Dikshan Gautam6 min read

Most small and mid-sized businesses in Nepal don't need a 40-point SEO checklist. They need three or four things done properly, in the right order. Here's where I'd start.

1. Make sure Google can actually find and read your site

Before worrying about rankings, check the basics: does your site load on a phone in a few seconds, is there one clear page for each main service or product, and can Google Search Console see your pages without errors? A surprising number of Nepali business websites fail on one of these before SEO even becomes relevant.

2. Research how your customers actually search

Search behaviour in Nepal is rarely pure English. People search in English, Nepali, and Romanized Nepali — often mixing all three in the same session. A business that only optimises for the English term is missing a real slice of demand. Spend time looking at the exact phrases people use, not just the "correct" industry term.

3. Fix on-page basics before writing new content

Titles, headings and descriptions that actually describe the page (not just the business name repeated everywhere), internal links between related pages, and one clear call to action per page. This is unglamorous work, but it's usually worth more than another blog post.

If you can only do one thing this month: check whether your homepage title and description actually describe what you do and where — most don't.

4. Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

For any business with a physical location or service area, this often matters more than the website itself. It's covered in detail in the Google Business Profile guide.

5. Build content around real questions, not keyword stuffing

Write the page you'd want to read if you were the customer trying to decide. Answer the actual question in the first two sentences, then add the detail. That single habit fixes more content problems than any tool.

What I'd avoid

  • Buying backlinks from unrelated, low-quality sites — it rarely helps and can actively hurt.
  • Publishing content nobody is searching for, just to "keep the blog active."
  • Ignoring page speed and mobile experience because "the site looks fine on my laptop."

SEO in Nepal isn't fundamentally different from SEO anywhere else — but the mix of local intent, language and a less mature competitive landscape means a small, focused effort can go further than in a saturated market.
